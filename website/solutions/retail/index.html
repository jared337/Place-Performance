Guide

Footfall analytics for shopping centres and precincts.

Footfall is the most basic measure of a retail destination's health. Combined with sales, dwell and zone data it becomes a powerful tool for leasing, marketing, operations and asset strategy.

Visitation by entrance, zone and hour

Entrance counters measure footfall by door, hour and day, while zone sensors show how visitors spread across malls, wings and food courts. Peak-period grids reveal the busiest hours so rosters, cleaning and security match demand. Read more about shopping centres.

Conversion and tenant performance

Point-of-sale integrations combine footfall with sales to calculate conversion and sales per visitor by zone. Ranking zones and tenants highlights strong and weak areas and supports leasing and rent reviews.

Events and campaigns

Comparing footfall, dwell and conversion during a promotion with the expected baseline shows the uplift it created. Scenario planning helps test changes such as new entrances or trading hours before committing.

Precincts and streets

Outdoor counting extends the picture to streets and laneways around a centre, useful for precinct managers and councils measuring town centre activity. Real-time alerts help manage queues at service desks and food courts.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How do shopping centres use footfall data?

Shopping centres use footfall data to understand visitation by entrance, zone and time, measure conversion with sales data, rank zones and tenants, staff for peaks, and evaluate the impact of events and campaigns.

Can footfall be combined with sales data?

Yes. Point-of-sale integrations combine visitation with sales to calculate conversion and sales per visitor by site and zone.

Can it measure queue wait times?

Yes. Queue sensors at service desks and food courts measure queue length and wait time against your targets, with alerts when targets are at risk.

Does it identify shoppers?

No. Counting is anonymous, with processing on the device and no images or personal information collected.

Can it measure the impact of a promotion or event?

Yes. Comparing footfall, dwell and conversion during a campaign with the expected baseline shows the uplift it created.
