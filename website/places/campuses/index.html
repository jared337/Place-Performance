Why measure

Bookings show intent. Sensors show reality.

Most campus space decisions are made from timetables and booking systems, which record what was reserved rather than what was used. Rooms booked for a class of eighty may regularly hold thirty, while popular study spaces overflow at exam time. Measuring actual occupancy closes that gap.

Utilisation room by room

Sensors measure how many people are in each lecture theatre, tutorial room, lab and study area throughout the day. Combined with capacity, that gives true utilisation by room, building and time of week, which timetabling and estates teams can use to right-size allocations. Read more about space utilisation for universities.

Helping students find space

Live occupancy on interactive floorplans can show students which study rooms and library areas have space right now, spreading demand across buildings. Campus libraries use the same data for staffing and opening hours.

Planning the estate

Semester-on-semester trends and demand forecasts show whether a new building is really needed, or whether better use of existing space would meet demand. All counting is anonymous, as explained in privacy by design.

Typical uses on campus

Estates teams use utilisation data to consolidate under-used rooms, timetabling teams to match class sizes to rooms, library teams to plan exam-period hours, and student services to publish live study space. Facilities teams use visit counts to schedule cleaning in high-traffic buildings, and safety teams use live occupancy for large lecture theatres and event spaces.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How do you measure space utilisation on a university campus?

Sensors count how many people are in each room or zone throughout the day. Comparing measured occupancy with each room's capacity and its bookings shows true utilisation by room, building and time of week.

Can students see which study spaces are free?

Yes. Live occupancy can be shown on floorplans so students and staff can see which study rooms and library areas have space right now.

Does campus people counting identify students?

No. Counting is processed on the device and only anonymous numbers are sent, with no images or personal information collected.

Can utilisation data support capital planning?

Yes. Trends and forecasts show whether more space is genuinely needed or existing space could be used better, before committing to new buildings.

Can it measure lecture theatres and labs as well as study spaces?

Yes. Any room or zone can be measured, from large lecture theatres to labs, tutorial rooms, libraries and informal study areas, with each one shown on its building floorplan.

How long before utilisation data is useful?

Live occupancy is available as soon as sensors are commissioned. Patterns become clear within a few weeks, and a full semester gives a reliable picture for timetabling and estate planning.
