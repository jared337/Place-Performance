Pedestrian counting for streets and laneways.

Measure pedestrian activity in streets, laneways, plazas and town centres, hour by hour, and see how places change over time.

Why measure

Busy streets are the sign of a healthy place.

Pedestrian activity is one of the clearest signs of a thriving town centre or precinct. Councils, place managers and business associations use it to understand how streets are used, measure the effect of events and investment, and support economic development.

Activity by hour, day and season

Sensors at key points count pedestrians and their direction, showing how activity varies through the day, across the week and between seasons. Peak-period views show the busiest times for cleaning, safety and events.

Events and change

Comparing activity during events, festivals or promotions with normal patterns shows their impact. Measuring before and after streetscape works shows how a project changed the way people use an area. Read more about counting in parks, trails and streets.

Connected to the wider precinct

Street counts sit alongside shopping centre footfall and park visitation in one platform, so precinct managers and councils can see the whole picture.

Typical uses in streets

Place managers report on town centre activity, economic development teams track the recovery or growth of precincts, event teams measure festival attendance, and transport planners use pedestrian volumes to prioritise crossings, footpath widening and lighting.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How are pedestrians counted on a street?

Sensors mounted on poles, awnings or facades count pedestrians passing a point and their direction, using technology chosen for each location.

Can it measure the impact of an event or streetscape project?

Yes. Comparing activity during an event, or before and after a project, with normal patterns shows the change it created.

Does street counting record images of people?

No. Counting is processed on the device and only anonymous counts are transmitted.

Can it count cyclists and vehicles too?

Yes. Cyclist and vehicle counting can be added at locations where it is needed.

How many counting points does a street need?

It depends on the questions you want to answer. A site survey identifies the points that capture the main pedestrian flows, often entrances to a precinct and key intersections.

Can street counts be compared with nearby centres?

Yes. Street counts sit alongside shopping centre footfall and park visitation in one platform, so the whole precinct can be compared.
