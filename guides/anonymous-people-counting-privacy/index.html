Anonymous people counting and privacy

People counting measures how many, not who. Here is how modern sensors count without identifying anyone, and why that matters.

Counting people and respecting their privacy are not in tension. Modern people counting is built to measure how many people are in a space, and never who they are.

Counting bodies, not identities

Depth and movement sensors detect a person as a shape in motion and add to a count. They do not recognise faces, read features or match anyone to a record. The output is a number, not an identity.

Processed on the device

Counting happens inside the sensor. Only anonymous totals leave it, so no images or personal information travel across the network or sit in a database to be lost or misused.

What is never collected

No faces, no photographs, no names, no device identifiers and no personal information. There is nothing that could identify an individual, which is what makes the approach suitable for public and sensitive places.

Why it matters

Councils, libraries, workplaces and retailers increasingly have to show that monitoring is proportionate and privacy-safe. Anonymous counting clears that bar, and often removes the need for the consent and data-handling that camera-based approaches require.

A selling point, not a hurdle

Because it carries far fewer privacy concerns, anonymous counting is easier to deploy in community spaces, easier to explain to residents and staff, and easier to pass procurement.

FAQ

Questions & answers

Does people counting identify individuals?

No. People are counted as anonymous shapes and movement. No faces, images or personal information are captured, and counting is processed on the device.

Is anonymous counting better for privacy than cameras?

Yes. Because no recognisable images are captured and nothing identifies an individual, it carries far fewer privacy concerns than camera-based approaches and is well suited to public spaces.

Do we need signage or consent?

Requirements vary by site and jurisdiction, but because no personal information is collected, anonymous counting generally involves far lighter obligations than identifying methods. Confirm specifics with your own privacy adviser.
