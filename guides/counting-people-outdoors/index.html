Counting people outdoors

Trails, streets, parks and events bring challenges that indoor counting does not. Here is how outdoor people counting works.

Counting people outdoors is a different problem from counting them at an indoor doorway. There is weather, often no power or network, wide open spaces, and a mix of pedestrians, cyclists and vehicles to tell apart.

Weather, power and connectivity

Outdoor sensors are weatherproof and can run on battery, solar and mobile data, so they work on remote trails, in reserves and at temporary sites where there is no fixed power or network.

Radar for open spaces

Radar sensing suits the outdoors. It works in all weather, sees through rain and glare, covers a wide area from a single mount, and detects people, cyclists and vehicles by their movement, with direction and speed.

People, cyclists and vehicles

On shared paths and streets, telling pedestrians, cyclists and vehicles apart matters. Multi-modal sensors separate them, which is what lets councils size shared paths and reduce conflict.

Temporary and event deployments

Because outdoor units need no fixed infrastructure, they can be installed for a festival, market or works period and removed afterwards, with live data throughout.

Accuracy outdoors

Open-air counting can undercount, especially in crowds, so accuracy comes from choosing the right sensor for the site and calibrating against manual counts, rather than quoting a single headline figure.

FAQ

Questions & answers

How do you count people where there is no power or internet?

Outdoor counters run on battery, solar and mobile data, so they work on remote trails, in reserves and at temporary sites with no fixed power or network.

Can you tell pedestrians, cyclists and vehicles apart?

Yes. Multi-modal sensors separate pedestrians, cyclists and vehicles, with direction and speed, which is what shared paths and streets need.

Does it work in all weather?

Yes. Outdoor sensors are weatherproof, and radar in particular works through rain, glare and darkness.
