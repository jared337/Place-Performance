How people counting works

From the sensor on the wall to the number on your dashboard, here is how modern, anonymous people counting actually works.

People counting replaces manual clickers and guesswork with sensors that measure how many people enter, leave and occupy a space. The best systems do this anonymously, counting people as shapes in motion rather than recording who they are.

Counting happens on the device

Each sensor processes what it sees on the spot. It detects a person, follows their movement across a line or through a zone, and adds to a count. Only the anonymous numbers leave the device, so no images or personal information are sent anywhere.

Shapes and movement, not faces

Depth and movement sensing recognises the shape of a person and the direction they travel. Because it does not rely on recognisable images, it avoids the privacy and lighting problems of camera-based approaches and keeps working in the dark.

From count to dashboard in seconds

Counts are sent to the platform over a single network or mobile connection and appear within seconds. From there they become live occupancy, peak periods, dwell time, heat maps and trends, with alerts when a space approaches a limit.

Indoors, outdoors and in any light

Different sites need different sensing. Overhead depth sensors suit entrances and indoor zones, while all-weather sensors count people, cyclists and vehicles outdoors, including temporary and single-event deployments on battery and mobile data.

Keeping the numbers reliable

Accuracy comes from good placement and calibration during installation, then continuous device health monitoring so a sensor that goes quiet is flagged early. That is the difference between a count you report on and one you second-guess.

FAQ

Questions & answers

Does people counting use cameras?

Not necessarily. Depth and movement sensors count people by shape without capturing recognisable images, and process the count on the device, so only anonymous numbers leave the sensor.

How does a people counter know direction?

Sensors track a person as they cross a line or move through a zone, so they record entries and exits separately and can measure flow between areas.

How quickly does the data update?

Counts usually appear in the platform within a few seconds, which is what makes live occupancy and capacity alerts possible.
