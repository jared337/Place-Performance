Street festivals & town centres

People counting for street festivals and town centres

Measure crowd numbers and movement along the strip, so you can report attendance to funders and sponsors, run the event safely and build the case for your town centre.

Book a demo

The challenge

Prove the return, keep crowds safe.

Event organisers, business districts and place managers are under constant pressure to prove that festivals, street closures and activations deliver real return, to councils, sponsors and levy-paying businesses. At the same time they must keep dense temporary crowds safe.

Without objective footfall and crowd data they rely on ticket sales, gut feel or expensive one-off surveys. Continuous counting turns “it felt busy” into attendance, dwell, spend correlation and crowd-density evidence.

What you can measure

The data you get from day one.

Total footfall

Attendance along the strip and through key points, by hour.

Peak times

When the crowd builds and where it concentrates.

Crowd density

People per square metre, for safety and flow.

Movement & flow

Direction, speed and how people move through the precinct.

Dwell time

How long visitors stay, a proxy for spend.

Before and after

Event days compared with a normal baseline, and control streets.

Repeat & multi-day

Visitors who return across event days.

Year on year

Growth or decline against previous editions.

Ways to use it

How event and place teams use the data.

Prove the economic case

Combine attendance, dwell and repeat visits with a before-and-after baseline and card-spend data, the standard method funders and sponsors want.

Report to levy payers

Give business-district members footfall as the core measure of vitality, and evidence that events and initiatives work.

Price sponsorship

Give sponsors audited attendance and exposure numbers to set and defend sponsorship pricing.

Keep crowds safe

Monitor crowd density in people per square metre with alert thresholds, so control teams act before a crush develops.

Show closures do not harm trade

Measure footfall through a pedestrianisation or street closure to reassure businesses and residents.

Resource to real peaks

Staff, clean and resource security and transport to actual crowd levels, not worst-case assumptions.

Business impact

Make more money, save money and improve experiences.

Make more money

  • Price and defend sponsorship on audited attendance and exposure.
  • Justify the events budget and build the business case for the town centre.
  • Support local traders and grant applications with evidence of visitation.
  • Evidence that a pedestrianised, busier street lifts trade.

Save money

  • Resource staffing, security, cleansing and transport to real peaks.
  • Replace expensive one-off surveys with continuous data.
  • Avoid over or under-provisioning stalls, toilets, parking and shuttles.
  • Plan infrastructure and stages to where people actually go.

Improve experiences

  • Keep crowds safe with live density and capacity awareness.
  • Manage pinch points and congestion, and improve the layout.
  • Program and place attractions where they have the most impact.
  • Protect the experience with aggregate counting that respects privacy.

FAQ

Questions & answers

Can you count large outdoor crowds?

Yes. Wide-area sensors count crowds across open streets and plazas, and run on battery, solar and mobile data where there is no fixed power.

Can it keep crowds safe in real time?

Yes. Crowd density in people per square metre, with alert thresholds, lets control teams act before conditions become unsafe.

Is footfall on its own enough?

It is the core measure, but the strongest cases pair it with dwell time, spend data and a comparison against a control street, so you show real impact, not just more people.

Can it be set up just for the festival?

Yes. Temporary deployments are installed for the event and removed afterwards, with live dashboards during the day.

Is this a privacy risk in a public space?

No. Aggregate, non-identifying counting avoids most privacy concerns, which is a key reason it is preferred for public events.
